Key Concept

Semantic validity is not statistical accuracy. It is structural completeness — every claim must satisfy six relational constraints (Referential, Contextual, Premissive, Inferential, Constraining, Teleological) before it can be emitted. A claim missing any constraint is incomplete, regardless of how confident the model is.

The Six Constraints Applied

Phase I — Domain applications of the semantic validity architecture.

Why Six?

A tetrahedron has exactly four vertices and six edges. The four vertices are the components of any claim: the Claimant, the Subject, the Grounds, and the Purpose. The six edges connecting them are the constraints that must all be satisfied for the claim to be semantically valid. This is not a design choice — it is a geometric necessity.
